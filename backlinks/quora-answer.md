## Option A — Question: "How do I calculate my macros for weight loss?"

**Post this as an answer:**

---

The short version: figure out your TDEE, then set a calorie deficit, then split those calories into protein, carbs, and fat based on your goal.

Here is the actual process:

**Step 1 — Calculate your BMR**
Your Basal Metabolic Rate is the calories your body burns at complete rest. The Mifflin-St Jeor formula is the most accurate for most people:

- Men: (10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) - (5 × age) + 5
- Women: (10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) - (5 × age) - 161

**Step 2 — Multiply by your activity level to get TDEE**

- Sedentary (desk job, no exercise): BMR × 1.2
- Light activity (1-3 days/week): BMR × 1.375
- Moderate (3-5 days/week): BMR × 1.55
- Very active (hard training 6-7 days): BMR × 1.725

**Step 3 — Set your deficit**
A 500 kcal/day deficit = roughly 1 lb/week of fat loss. 250 kcal/day is more sustainable and preserves more muscle.

**Step 4 — Set your macros**
For a fat loss goal:

- Protein: 0.8–1g per lb of bodyweight (muscle preservation)
- Fat: 0.35–0.45g per lb of bodyweight (hormonal health)
- Carbs: fill the remaining calories

Doing all of this by hand is tedious. I use [CalMacroCal](https://calmacrocal.com) — it runs Mifflin-St Jeor and Harris-Benedict side by side, accounts for your activity level, and spits out macro splits for cutting, bulking, strength, or endurance in about 10 seconds. No account, no email, free.

The key thing most people miss is that your TDEE changes as you lose weight — recalculate every 4-6 weeks.

## Option B — Question: "What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?"

**Post this as an answer:**

---

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the calories your body burns just to stay alive — breathing, circulation, organ function. Think of it as your engine idling in park.

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. It represents the total calories you actually burn in a day, including movement, exercise, and the thermic effect of food.

**Why does this matter?**

- Eat at your TDEE: maintain weight
- Eat below your TDEE: lose weight
- Eat above your TDEE: gain weight

That is all of nutrition in one sentence.

**How to calculate both:**

Your BMR is calculated using a formula — Mifflin-St Jeor is the current gold standard. Your TDEE is BMR × your activity multiplier (ranges from 1.2 for sedentary to 1.9 for very hard training + physical job).

If you want to skip the math, [CalMacroCal](https://calmacrocal.com) calculates both instantly and then shows you macro targets broken down by goal (cut, bulk, strength, endurance). Nothing to sign up for.

The common mistake: people calculate their TDEE once and never update it. As your weight changes, your BMR changes. Recalculate every month or after significant weight change.

## Option C — Question: "What macros should I eat to build muscle?"

**Post this as an answer:**

---

Building muscle requires two things at the nutrition level: enough protein to support muscle protein synthesis, and enough total calories to fuel training and recovery. Everything else is secondary.

**The baseline targets:**

- **Protein:** 0.7–1g per lb of bodyweight (higher end if you are advanced or in a calorie deficit)
- **Calories:** 200–500 above your TDEE (a lean bulk — more than this adds unnecessary fat)
- **Carbs:** prioritize around training for performance and glycogen replenishment
- **Fat:** minimum 0.35g per lb of bodyweight — do not go lower, hormonal production requires it

**Practical example (180 lb male, moderate activity):**

- TDEE: ~2,700 kcal
- Lean bulk target: ~3,000 kcal
- Protein: 180g (720 kcal)
- Fat: 70g (630 kcal)
- Carbs: fill remaining ~1,650 kcal = ~412g

The number one error I see: people hit protein targets but eat at maintenance or below. You cannot build muscle in a meaningful deficit long-term.
