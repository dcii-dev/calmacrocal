## Article: How to Calculate Your Macros (Without a Spreadsheet or a Nutrition Degree)

**Suggested tags:** Nutrition, Fitness, Weight Loss, Health, Muscle Building

---

Most people know they should "track macros" but have no idea where the numbers come from. This is the actual math — and a faster way to get there.

### Step 1: Calculate Your BMR

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest — just keeping your heart beating, lungs breathing, and organs running. Everything starts here.

The two most widely used formulas:

**Mifflin-St Jeor** (current clinical standard):

- Men: (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) + 5
- Women: (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) - 161

**Harris-Benedict** (older, slightly higher estimates):

- Men: (13.397 × kg) + (4.799 × cm) - (5.677 × age) + 88.362
- Women: (9.247 × kg) + (3.098 × cm) - (4.330 × age) + 447.593

The formulas produce slightly different numbers. Neither is "correct" — they are estimates. Running both and taking the midpoint is a reasonable approach.

### Step 2: Multiply by Your Activity Level to Get TDEE

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your real-world calorie burn — BMR adjusted for how active you actually are.

| Activity Level                             | Multiplier |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| Sedentary (desk job, no exercise)          | 1.2        |
| Lightly active (1-3 days/week)             | 1.375      |
| Moderately active (3-5 days/week)          | 1.55       |
| Very active (hard training 6-7 days/week)  | 1.725      |
| Extremely active (physical job + training) | 1.9        |

Most people overestimate their activity level. When in doubt, go one tier lower and adjust based on real results.

### Step 3: Set a Calorie Target Based on Your Goal

- **Fat loss:** TDEE minus 300-500 kcal/day. Larger deficits lose muscle faster.
- **Lean bulk:** TDEE plus 200-300 kcal/day. Slow gains, minimal fat.
- **Maintenance/recomp:** Eat at TDEE, prioritize protein and training stimulus.

### Step 4: Split Your Calories Into Macros

**For fat loss:**

- Protein: 0.8-1g per lb of bodyweight (protects muscle in a deficit)
- Fat: minimum 0.35g per lb (hormonal function)
- Carbs: remaining calories

**For muscle building:**

- Protein: 0.7-1g per lb of bodyweight
- Fat: 0.4-0.5g per lb
- Carbs: fill the rest (fuel for training)

**For endurance athletes:**

- Carbs become the priority — up to 50-60% of total calories
- Protein: 0.6-0.8g per lb
- Fat: minimum 0.3g per lb

### The Most Common Mistakes

1. **Calculating TDEE once and never updating it.** As your weight changes, your BMR changes. Recalculate every 4-6 weeks or after a 5+ lb change.
2. **Overestimating activity level.** This inflates TDEE and wipes out the deficit.
3. **Hitting macros but ignoring food quality.** 180g of protein from processed sources performs differently than whole food sources. Macros are a framework, not a permission slip.
4. **Abandoning the approach after two weeks.** Body composition changes happen on a 6-12 week timeline, not a 2-week one.
